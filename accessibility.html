Digital Inclusion

Inclusion is intentional.

Rest should be available to everyone — regardless of ability, language, or device. Below is a plain account of how this site is built to lower barriers.

If something isn't working for you, the contact at the bottom of this page goes directly to the people who can fix it.

I.

Language Access

The site translates into seven languages without a page reload or third-party plugin. Select your language from the globe icon in the top navigation — your preference is saved and applied automatically on every return visit.

EN  ·  ES  ·  FR  ·  DE  ·  PT  ·  JA  ·  ZH

II.

Sensory Control

The Atmosphere layer — ambient audio, particle effects, and enhanced animation — is off by default. A clearly labelled toggle gives full control at any time. The site also respects the browser's prefers-reduced-motion setting, suppressing motion without any action required from you.

III.

Visual Clarity

Our target is WCAG 2.1 Level AA — a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for body text and 3:1 for large text and UI components. All gold-on-black and cream-on-black pairings have been reviewed. Every interactive element has a visible focus state.

IV.

Assistive Technology

Pages are structured with semantic HTML5 — headings, landmarks, lists, and buttons are all correct. A skip-to-content link appears on first Tab press. All decorative SVGs carry aria-hidden. If JavaScript is unavailable, a fallback stylesheet restores scroll, native cursor, and full content visibility.

V.

Inclusive Language

All clinical copy uses "provider" rather than "physician." This reflects the full spectrum of licensed care — physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants — without implying a particular type of clinical relationship.

Feedback is a gift.

If something on this site creates a barrier — a contrast issue, a focus trap, a translation error, anything at all — we want to know immediately. Your report is the fastest path to a fix.
